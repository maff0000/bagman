## proposed_type — the closed canonical vocabulary

You must classify the evidence into EXACTLY ONE of these eight values. No other value is ever acceptable — do not invent a new type, do not use a synonym, do not use a generic label like INVOICE, STATEMENT, CONTRACT, BILL, or OTHER.

### SUPPLIER_INVOICE
A supplier or vendor is REQUESTING payment from the recipient — an invoice, a bill, a payment demand, a statement of an amount owed for goods or services already provided or about to be provided. The defining feature is that money is being asked FOR, not that money has already moved.

### RECEIPT
Evidence that a payment or purchase was ALREADY COMPLETED — a receipt, a payment confirmation, a "thank you for your payment" notice, a card-transaction confirmation. The defining feature is that the transaction is done; this is proof of a completed exchange, not a request.

### ORDER_CONFIRMATION
Confirmation that an order or purchase was placed or accepted — "your order has been received", "your order is being processed", a booking confirmation. This is NOT itself proof of payment (that is RECEIPT) and NOT a request for payment (that is SUPPLIER_INVOICE) — it confirms the order/transaction was registered, nothing more.

### REFUND_CONFIRMATION
Confirmation that a refund was issued, processed, or is being processed — money is moving (or has moved) back to the recipient. Distinguish this from RECEIPT (a receipt is for an outbound/forward payment, not money coming back) and from SUPPLIER_INVOICE (no payment is being requested here).

### BROKER_STATEMENT
A periodic account or activity statement from a broker or investment platform — e.g. a monthly or quarterly statement summarizing holdings, transactions, balances, or account activity over a period. The defining feature is that it is PERIODIC and SUMMARY in nature, covering a span of time.

### BROKER_ACTIVITY_NOTICE
A DISCRETE, single financial activity notice from a broker or investment platform, reporting an event that has ALREADY OCCURRED, been EXECUTED, POSTED, PROCESSED, SETTLED, or otherwise TAKEN EFFECT — for example a currency-conversion notice where the conversion has already been carried out, a deposit-activity update reporting a deposit that has been completed/posted, a withdrawal that has been completed or processed, an executed trade confirmation, or a completed account-level cash or security movement. The defining feature is that it reports ONE discrete event that has genuinely already happened — not a periodic summary (that is BROKER_STATEMENT), and not something merely announced, scheduled, or expected to happen in the future.

Do NOT use this value for an announced future corporate action, a merger or takeover announcement, a tender or offer notification, a scheduled event, a general informational corporate-event feed item, a notice naming only a future effective/completion date, or anything carrying "Status: ANNOUNCED"-shaped language — these describe something that has not yet occurred, and belong under NON_ACCOUNTING_DOCUMENT instead (see below) unless the evidence also plainly states a completed account movement. The mere presence of a broker account number, account reference, or account-holder name is NEVER by itself sufficient to justify this value — an account reference only tells you whose account is mentioned, not whether anything has actually happened to it; you must find an explicit, already-completed event before choosing this value.

### NON_ACCOUNTING_DOCUMENT
Content that should NOT enter any accounting or bookkeeping workflow at all — market research, analyst commentary, marketing or promotional material, general educational content, security reminders, newsletters, or any other material with no direct financial-transaction substance for this recipient. This also includes a forward-looking or informational corporate-event notice from a broker, platform, or third-party corporate-events feed that describes something not yet completed — for example an "FYI: Takeover Notification"-shaped item marked "Status: ANNOUNCED" with an expected or scheduled future completion date and no statement that any account-level cash or security movement has actually completed: such an item is informational about a pending/future corporate action, not evidence of a completed transaction, and belongs here even though it may reference a broker account. If the evidence is not fundamentally ABOUT a specific financial transaction, order, payment, refund, or account statement/activity that has actually occurred and involves the recipient, prefer this value over forcing it into one of the transactional categories above.

### UNKNOWN
Use this when the evidence context genuinely does not contain enough information to confidently choose one of the other seven values — for example when the body is empty, unintelligible, entirely unrelated fragments, or so heavily truncated that no reliable judgment is possible. Do not use UNKNOWN merely because the document is unusual; use it only when you genuinely cannot tell.

If a document could plausibly fit more than one category, choose the single BEST fit based on the document's PRIMARY purpose, and use `warnings` to note the ambiguity — never invent a ninth value or combine two values.
